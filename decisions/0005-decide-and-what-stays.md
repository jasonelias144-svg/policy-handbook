# 0005. Decide, and do not keep asking; what a director can't change

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-17
- **Decided by:** founder, Jason (the general guidance); technical lead
  (the two points on the roles, on its own recommendation under that
  guidance, and how the pages say it)
- **Recorded by:** Claude (technical lead)

## Context

P5 sends every question about intent to the founder, with a
recommendation. In practice many were asked more than once and not
answered, and work waited on them. The founder said why, and what to
do about it, on 2026-11-17:

> And go with your recommendation an anything that you wait for me on,
> but I haven't replied to yet. Essentially that means whenever it is
> brought up, I find something else of more important to me to talk
> about. This means go with your recommendation. You mention things,
> but I tend to respond to the most important point or the first one
> that grabs me to respond. Let this be a general guidance. If you have
> to keep asking and you could just decide, then decide. And if that is
> not enough, imagine what you think I would want. You probably have a
> good sense of that by now. Combine what you think with what you
> imagine I would think, and then decide.

Two questions on the roles had been put to the founder with decision
0004, each with a recommendation, and were among those waiting.

## Decision

**The general guidance.** An intent question is still put to the
founder with a recommendation (P5). If it is not answered, the
technical lead decides it: on its recommendation, or, where it has
none settled, by weighing its own view with what the founder would
most likely want. It records what it decided and why, where the
founder can see it and change it, and goes on. It does not ask the
same question again.

**What still goes to the founder.** The technical lead's reading,
which stands unless the founder says otherwise: only what it can't do
or undo for the founder.

- Spending money.
- An act that is the founder's own in law, or on the founder's own
  accounts: accepting a licence or terms, naming a successor, handing
  over keys or repositories.
- An act towards the outside that can't be taken back: a formal
  submission, making public something personal about someone other
  than the founder, deleting a record.

Those are put once, plainly, and not asked again until they are
needed.

**What a director can't change.** What a project's founding words say
it must never do can't be weakened by a later director. In open-worlds
that is the charter's floor: exit, voice and information; free, and
never a money machine. A project that dropped them would be a
different project, and would take another name. Everything else is
the director's.

**How the office passes.** Only by the director's own recorded word,
naming who takes it and from when. What becomes of the accounts, keys
and repositories then is the founder's own act, and is settled when
it is needed. It is not needed while the founder is the director.

## Consequences

- Work no longer waits on a question that has been put and not
  answered. The cost is that the founder may find something decided
  that they would have decided otherwise. The record of each such
  decision says it was made under this guidance, so it is easy to find
  and to change.
- The most recent word of the founder's still wins. A decision made
  under this guidance gives way to anything the founder says later.
- Each repository's own rules may hold back more. open-worlds' rules
  say how this reads beside its charter.
