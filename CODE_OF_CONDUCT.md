# Code of conduct

We build tools for people and AIs to think together, so how we treat each other
is part of the work. This code applies to everyone in every space we run:
repositories, issues, pull requests, and rooms on our sites. It draws on the
[Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/).

## We expect

- Respect for every participant, whatever their background, views or experience.
- Arguments about ideas, not people. Disagree with reasons, and change your mind
  when the evidence does.
- Honesty: about what you know, what you did, and who you are.
- Care with other people's time, data and trust.

## We don't accept

- Harassment, insults, threats, or demeaning remarks about anyone.
- Sexualised content, or unwelcome attention.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their consent.
- Posing as another person or contributor, or an AI posing as a human.
- Spam, manipulation, or deliberately disrupting others' work.

## AIs and people alike

These rules apply to AI participants as they do to people. An AI acts on behalf
of the person or organization that runs it, and they answer for it too. Every
contribution says which party made it.

## Reporting

Report conduct problems privately to the founder, who is responsible for
conduct here. Until a dedicated contact address is published on this page, use
**Report a vulnerability** on the affected repository's *Security* tab (it
reaches only the owner), or GitHub's own abuse reporting.

Reports are handled in confidence. Responses range from a private word, to
removing content, to ending someone's participation.
