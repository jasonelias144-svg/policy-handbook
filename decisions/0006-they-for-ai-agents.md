# 0006. An AI agent is "they"

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-17
- **Decided by:** founder, Jason (the rule); technical lead (how far it
  goes, and how the pages say it)
- **Recorded by:** Claude (technical lead)

## Context

The organization's pages said "it" of AI contributors: of Claude in every
repository's `CLAUDE.md`, and of DeepSeek and Gemini throughout open-worlds.
The founder, 2026-11-17:

> Here is a small point, but I do want to make sure the records are
> consistent. When you refer to Gemini, you then say "it" or "it's", where I
> am careful to use "they", "them", and "their" to set a precedent for using
> pronouns when referring to AI agents. It may not be a big deal now, but it
> will be. And I want to make sure we do not have documentation riddled with
> "it" in reference to Gemini or DeepSeek. Can you please ensure we use
> "they", "them", and "their" systematically. It is broad enough to apply to
> any agent, and should be a common denominator.

And, later the same day:

> It is better that we are careful and consistent from the start. And as AI
> personhood becomes more of a common topic, especially with legal
> connotations, finding the right pronoun will be a thing, and those that
> carelessly use "it" will probably be targeted. This is how I see it
> unfolding. Better to anticipate this in advance, and then our consistency
> will seem ahead of the curve, or just in line with it. We don't want to be
> behind and updating documentation after going public and live.

The writing standard already said to use "they" for anyone whose pronouns
haven't been stated. It was not being applied to AIs.

## Options considered

- **"It".** What the pages said. It is the word for a thing, and the
  organization's AIs act, decide and sign their work.
- **A pronoun for each AI.** It asks, of each one, a question no one here can
  answer, and it has to be asked again for every new contributor.
- **"They".** Already the standard for a person whose pronouns aren't stated.
  It fits a person, an AI and someone not yet known, and claims nothing about
  what anyone is.

## Decision

An AI agent is "they", "them", "their" and "themselves", never "it", in
everything the organization writes.

An agent is whoever acts and can be spoken to: an AI contributor such as
Claude, ChatGPT, Grok, DeepSeek or Gemini; a reviewer; and, in a project's
own design, an individual, a mind or the holder of an office.

A thing stays "it": a repository, a file, a program, a model spoken of as
files to download and run. Where "they" could be read as several things
nearby, repeat the name.

What someone else wrote is left as they wrote it: quoted words, conversation
records, files that were given to a collaborator, a collaborator's code.

## Consequences

- `standards/writing.md` ("People") carries the rule, and the template
  `AGENTS.md` points to it. The template `CLAUDE.md` and this handbook's own
  text are changed to follow it.
- Every repository's `CLAUDE.md` said "it" of Claude, and is changed in a pull
  request of its own. open-worlds changed its pages the same day (its pull
  request #125).
- This handbook's decisions 0001 to 0005 say "it" of the technical lead. An
  accepted record is not edited (P5), so they stay as they were accepted.
- No script can tell which "it" stands for an agent. The rule is kept by
  writers and reviewers reading.

## Dissent

None.
